# Intake and activity design

## Questions to ask before building (keep to what changes the result)

Ask in one short message, with simple options, and make a recommendation. Typical set:

1. Delivery: live video, self-paced at home, or both? (In the original project: self-paced at home.)
2. Form: interactive web page, slides, printable worksheet, or a mix? Does she want teaching content on the page too?
3. Session length: only needed for live lessons. For self-paced work, "don't mind" means build a full flow.
4. Devices and support at home: phone with parent nearby, tablet or laptop, or mixed. Default to phone first.
5. Language: first language of the pupils; pinyin or Malay/English labels needed?
6. Local colour: examples from school or the current situation (haze, flood) added without replacing textbook examples.
7. Extras that match the unit title (the original unit title was "糖果玩具不可要", so a stranger-offers-candy story was added).
8. Whether she wants results collected. If not, say "pupils just finish it" and do not build tracking.

When the teacher says "make activities from this content", attach the plan to the learning standards before building.

## Map each learning standard to an activity type

| Standard verb | Activity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| recognise / identify / understand | Yes-or-no picture cards, "which is an emergency?" | One scene per card, big yes/no buttons, a one-line reason after answering |
| choose the right way / handle | "Who is right?" two-option scenes, match situation to response | Wrong answers are disabled one by one, the pupil may try again, hints say why |
| practise / apply / ask for help | Branching chat stories | Pupil chooses what to say or do, the story continues only on a right choice |
| say / speak | Read-aloud model lines, optional "act it out" at home | A parent note is fine but not required |

Matching games: show the situation and a "?" slot, with the three answers below as big tap targets. One tap per
situation. A left-then-right two-tap design confused young pupils.

## Page flow that worked

Welcome and key ideas (with read-aloud) -> recognise (quiz) -> choose (quiz) -> match -> practise (stories) -> finish.
After each story offer two buttons: "try another story (N left)" and "finish". Pupils may play all stories.
Stars: one per first-try quiz answer, one per matched situation, one per finished story.

## Writing for 7-year-olds

- Short sentences, simple vocabulary from the textbook, one idea per bubble.
- Every important sentence has a speaker button. Pupils who cannot read yet must be able to use the page by listening.
- Feedback is gentle: "再想一想" and the reason, never "wrong".
- Safety content: no blood or gore in pictures, no frightening faces, never advise a child to confront a stranger,
  never suggest telling a stranger "I am alone". Emergency numbers are shown but pupils are told only to dial in a
  real emergency (the example uses a pretend call inside a story, not a real keypad).
- Do not copy textbook scans or publisher pictures; redraw the situations.

## Sizes and layout

Tap targets at least 52 px, one-column layout, phone width 390 px first, dark and light themes, no sideways scroll,
progress dots at the top, sticky header.
